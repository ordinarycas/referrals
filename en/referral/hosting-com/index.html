What is shared (virtual) hosting?

Shared hosting, also called virtual hosting, means a hosting company splits one physical server into many separate spaces and rents each one to a different customer. Because the hardware cost is shared, it is far cheaper than running your own server, which is why most site owners rent hosting instead.

If your domain name is the street address, hosting is the building behind it that holds your site's files. When a site outgrows shared hosting, the usual next steps are a VPS (a virtual server with its own reserved resources) or a dedicated server.

What do I need to build a website?

You need three things: a domain name, hosting and the site itself.

  • Domain name: the address visitors type to reach your site.
  • Hosting: the server space that stores your site's files and data. You rent it.
  • Site design and content: built with a platform such as WordPress, or coded by hand.

A typical WordPress setup runs in this order: decide what the site is for, pick a domain and hosting, install WordPress, set up SSL and point the domain at the hosting, then design the pages, publish and keep the site maintained. Buying the domain and hosting from the same company usually saves you the separate step of pointing the domain at the server. Hosting.com's shared plans list one-click WordPress install and a free domain for the first year.

How do I choose a domain name, and should I pick .com or .tw?

Keep it short, easy to remember and easy to type or share.

  • Use your brand name, or a word tied to your product or service.
  • English or romanized names are easier to use than Chinese domains, which some systems show as a Punycode string of letters and digits.
  • The address works with or without www. Pick one version as the main one and redirect the other to it.

For the ending (top-level domain), .com is the safe default: widely recognized and open to any use. Country-code domains such as .tw, .com.tw, .cn or .jp suit sites aimed at one market. .co is often used as a fallback when the .com is taken (it is actually Colombia's country code). .net, .org and .info suggest network services, organizations and information sites, but are usually open to anyone. If the name you want is taken, a different ending often still works.

How do I choose a hosting plan?

Compare these points in order:

  • Company and service: track record, support channels and hours, backups and security.
  • Performance and specs: storage type (NVMe SSD is faster than a traditional hard disk), number of sites allowed, CPU and memory limits, caching.
  • Data center location: the closer to your visitors, the lower the latency usually is.
  • Price: look beyond the first-term price to the renewal price, storage and traffic limits, and whether backups, email, SSL and a CDN cost extra.
  • Room to grow: whether you can move up to a VPS or dedicated server and how painful a migration would be.

No provider is best at everything. Write down where your visitors are, your expected traffic and your budget, then compare against this list.
